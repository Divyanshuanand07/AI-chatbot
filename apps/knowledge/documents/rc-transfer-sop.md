## What RC transfer is

The registration certificate records the legal owner of the vehicle. Transfer
of ownership from the seller to the buyer is filed with the Regional Transport
Office. Until it completes, the buyer is driving a vehicle registered in
someone else's name, which is why the status matters to customers.

## When it is filed

The RC transfer is filed once the vehicle payment is settled and the
seller-side documents are complete. Filing before payment is settled is not
permitted.

## Statuses

- NOT_STARTED — no application filed. If the order status says RC transfer is
  pending while the vehicle shows not started, someone has missed the filing.
  This is an internal miss, not an RTO delay.
- APPLIED — submitted to the RTO and awaiting processing.
- IN_PROGRESS — the RTO has begun processing.
- COMPLETED — ownership updated. The customer should receive the new RC by
  post from the RTO directly.
- REJECTED — the RTO refused the filing.

## SLA and follow-up

The standard expectation is **twenty-one days** from filing to completion,
though this varies materially by state. Past twenty-one days:

1. Follow up with the assigned RTO agent for that hub.
2. Give the customer a revised date rather than repeating the original one.
3. Record the follow-up against the order so the next agent does not repeat it.

Delivery is not blocked by a pending RC transfer where local regulations allow
it, but the customer must be told in writing that the transfer is still in
progress and given an expected date.

## Common rejection reasons

- Seller signature mismatch on Form 29 or Form 30.
- Loan NOC missing where the vehicle was hypothecated.
- Insurance lapsed at the time of filing.
- Mismatch between chassis number on the documents and the vehicle.
- Pending traffic challans against the vehicle.

## Handling a rejection

An RTO rejection is a re-filing task, not a customer follow-up. Obtain the
rejection memo, correct the specific defect, and re-file. Do not ask the
customer for documents they have already provided unless the defect is
genuinely in their paperwork — re-requesting documents is the most common
complaint on rejected filings.

Where the rejection is due to a lapsed insurance policy or a pending challan,
resolve it centrally and bill it under the order's charges rather than asking
the customer to deal with the RTO.
